# Lunch and Behavioral

Reported: "an informal lunch interview where they ask you generic questions." Informal ≠
not evaluated. They're asking themselves: *Would I want this person sitting next to me
during a stressful trading day? Are they curious? Are they honest?*

Prep these out loud, **not word-for-word.** Bullet points in your head, natural delivery.

---

## 1. "Tell me about yourself" (60 seconds)
Template:
1. **Now:** what you study / where you are.
2. **What you've done** that's relevant: 1–2 concrete things (a project, a job, debugging
   something real, anything where you dealt with live data or pressure).
3. **Why here:** one sentence connecting it to TDOE.

Write your version here:
```
-
-
-
```

## 2. "Why TDOE instead of software engineering?" (reported question, have this ready)
Ingredients to pick from (use what's actually true for you):
- You like being **close to the trading**: seeing the impact of your work in real time,
  not months later.
- You like **problem solving under real conditions**: figuring out why something broke
  *right now*, with real consequences.
- The **mix**: part technical, part markets, part people. You talk to traders and
  developers both.
- **Ownership** during the trading day. You're the one who makes sure it works.
- You've noticed you enjoy **debugging and understanding systems** as much as building them.

Avoid: "coding is too hard", "it seemed easier to get". Frame it as wanting *this*, not
avoiding the other.

## 3. "Why Jane Street?"
Pick 2 that are true for you:
- Teaching culture: they sent you a primer and an Intro to Python. They clearly invest in
  people learning.
- Collaborative, low-ego reputation; people work in teams on the trading floor.
- Real trading at serious scale: the systems you'd support matter.
- Something specific from a conversation in an earlier round. **This is the strongest
  answer if you have one.** Mention the person or topic.

## 4. Short behavioral stories (have 3 ready, ~1 min each)
Use **Situation → what *you* did → result → what you learned.**

| Prompt | Your story (fill in) |
|---|---|
| A time you made a mistake | |
| A time you debugged something hard / figured out why something was wrong | |
| A time you worked under pressure or a deadline | |
| A time you had to explain something technical to a non-technical person | |
| A time you disagreed with someone | |

For the **mistake** one: own it fully, show what you did to fix it, and say what you changed
so it wouldn't happen again. TDOEs make mistakes under pressure; they want to see you
escalate honestly and learn, not hide it.

## 5. Questions to ask THEM (have 4, use 2–3)
- "What does a typical trading day look like for a TDOE? What's the most stressful part?"
- "What's something an intern built or fixed last summer that's still being used?"
- "How do TDOEs and the traders/developers split responsibility when something breaks?"
- "What separates an intern who does well here from one who just does okay?"
- "What was the last really interesting problem you debugged?" (People love answering this.)
- "How did you learn the trading side when you started?"

## 6. Lunch etiquette (quick)
- Order something easy to eat. Don't order the most expensive thing.
- Be curious about *them*. Ask follow-up questions on what they say.
- It's fine to talk about non-work things (hobbies, where you're from). Be a normal,
  friendly person.
- Don't badmouth anything (previous jobs, school, other firms).
- If they ask about the earlier rounds, be honest: "I think the order book one went well; I
  got stuck on X for a while in the coding round and would do it differently now."

## 7. "Do you have anything else to add?" (end of day)
One sentence: why you'd be excited to do this job + one thing you enjoyed about today.
