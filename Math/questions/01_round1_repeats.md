> What's your average speed?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q1.2</summary>

## Q2. Dice vs. two other players · ⏱ 10 min

> "You and two other players each roll a fair die. The highest roll wins. Ties are rerolled.
> What's the probability that you win?"

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q2.1</summary>

> "The other two have both already rolled a 4. You haven't rolled yet. What's your probability
> of winning now?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q2.2</summary>

> "Now only one opponent has rolled, and it's a 4. The other hasn't rolled yet. Your probability?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q2.3</summary>

> "What about n players?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q2.4</summary>

> "You roll first and see your number before deciding whether to play. It costs $X to play and the
> winner gets $Y. When do you play?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q2.5</summary>

> "Switching gears: what's the probability that three dice show no tie at all?"

</details>

## Q4. Bus: run or walk? · ⏱ 10 min

> "A bus comes every 20 minutes. The stop is 5 minutes away if you run and 10 minutes away if you
> walk. You don't know the schedule. What's your expected wait at the stop if you run? If you walk?"

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q4.1</summary>

> "What's the expected total time from now until you're on the bus, running vs. walking?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q4.2</summary>

> "Now you know the schedule: buses at :00, :20, :40. When should you run?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q4.3</summary>

> "Say you only care about the total time until you board. You don't mind whether you spend it
> walking or waiting at the stop. Does your answer change?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q4.4</summary>

> "Running costs you the equivalent of c minutes of effort. When is it worth it, with a known
> schedule and with an unknown one?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q4.5</summary>

> "Now buses arrive as a Poisson process, on average every 20 minutes. What's your expected wait?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q4.6</summary>

> "The gaps between buses are random, with some distribution X. What's your expected wait?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q4.7</summary>

> "You arrive just as a bus pulls away. What's your expected wait, with a fixed schedule vs. Poisson?"

</details>

## Q5. Hiring a CEO · ⏱ 10–15 min

> "You're hiring a CEO. There are 3 candidates, interviewed one at a time in random order. After each
> interview you must hire that person on the spot or reject them forever. You can only rank candidates
> against the ones you've already seen. You want the best one. What's your strategy, and how often
> does it work?"

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q5.1</summary>

> "Change the goal: you only need a *competent* CEO. Each candidate is independently competent with
> probability p, and you can tell at the interview whether they are. What's your strategy, and what's
> your success probability?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q5.2</summary>

> "Now you *can't* observe competence directly. You only see who's better than whom, and competent
> candidates always outrank incompetent ones. Each candidate is still competent with probability p.
> Compare 'hire the first one' with your strategy from the main question."

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q5.3</summary>

> "Candidates A, B, C are competent with probability 70%, 50%, and 30%, and you can observe
> competence. You pick the interview order. Does the order matter?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q5.4</summary>

> "Candidate i is good with probability pᵢ, is worth vᵢ if good, and costs cᵢ to interview. You hire
> the first good one. In what order should you interview them?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q5.5</summary>

> "Back to the original setup, but with n candidates. What's your strategy, and what happens to the
> success probability as n grows?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q5.6</summary>

> "Your success probability for large n is lower than what you got with 3. Isn't that worse?"

</details>

<details>
<summary>Follow-up Q5.7</summary>

> "Exactly one of the 3 candidates is competent. You can run noisy tests on them. How do you proceed?
> For example, a test that's 80% accurate says A is competent. What's the probability A really is?"

</details>
